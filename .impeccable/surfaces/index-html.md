## Direction contract

THESIS: The opening is Iliya's name made into a built object: solid letters standing on a drafting grid, with small interface parts on and beside them and one block hopping from letter to letter, so the first thing a visitor sees is a designer who builds things, with his name as the thing built. It refuses the centred headline with decorative props on either side, the split hero, and the slogan over a photo.

OWN-WORLD: Graphite night ground (oklch 15% 0.01 270) with soft raised panels one step lighter, warm-white text, and one accent: light purple (hue set once in the token root). On night the highlight is lavender (about oklch 81% 0.11): the primary button with graphite text, links, focus, active marks and the light faces of the 3D parts; on paper a deeper violet (oklch 54%) carries links and focus for contrast. Warm paper with carbon ink for About and the case-study body. Four root tokens (hue, radius 10px capped on controls, width 100%, density 1); nothing on the page changes them. Mona Sans 620 normal width for the name and headings; Martian Mono only for times and indices. Hairline borders, no glow, no gradient panels, no glass on cards; translucency only on the small 3D parts.

STORY: The visitor meets the name as an object being assembled, reads the one-line thesis and role under it, sees three practices with proof, opens Plater and follows one order through the product, then reads the case study on paper and writes to him.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Nav: wordmark, live "Sofia hh:mm", Work, About, Contact, CV. The first screen is a stage above a foot row. Centre stage: "Iliya Dinev" in Mona Sans 620 at −0.03em, laid on an axonometric plane (rotateX 56°, rotateZ −30°, no perspective) and standing 0.21em tall: the walls are one fine text-shadow stack in the word's own plane that projects straight down on screen, banded into a light-purple rim, a violet wall and a darker foot under a warm-white face. It stands on a drafting grid one step lighter than the night (graphite raised), with faint accent hairlines and a stronger line every fourth cell, larger than the word, which takes a visible cast shadow: a tight contact shade at the foot of the letters and a long soft one down and to the right. Parts: a glass card on the bowl of the a, a small glass-and-solid toggle lying on the grid in front of the e (clear of the line of letters, so it never reads as punctuation), and a small solid block hopping letter to letter; nothing rests on the D or the n. Foot row, left (columns 1–9): "I design for people with thirty seconds to spare." in lede size, then "Product designer in Sofia and the only designer at Valora, where I design Plater, a restaurant platform." as one grey line on desktop; right (10–12): square "See the work" (light purple, graphite text) and "Email me". One visually hidden h1 carries the name; the build is aria-hidden. The word scales with width and height (min(16vw, 26svh) up to 17rem; 19vw on tablet and phone); on tall screens the opening hugs its content instead of the full screen height; the foot row stacks below 960px.

FORM: "Your name, built", picked by the user from the second hand of three suggestions (four-to-two phones, before/after drag, the name built), position 3 of 3 on that list; seed: none (no concept roll was run: the options were shaped directly as a section inside the established Theme Room world, so there is no seed to cite). Signature: the name as real type on an axonometric plane with shadow-stack walls, on a lit grid with its cast shadow. Motion: on load the board tilts up into place (rotateX +14° to 0 over about one second) so the walls appear to rise as it settles, and the parts fade in at about half a second; then a slow nod about the horizontal axis only (55.5° to 57°, 14s) so the walls stay vertical, one shared 13.4s cycle: the block hops letter by letter from the I to the second i and back (I, l, i's tittle, y's arm, a's stem, D's corner, second tittle; landing squares measured on the rendered word), low arcs scaled to each distance and capped at 0.21em, a short crouch before take-off and a squash on landing, longer rests at both ends; the card on the a lifts 0.14em and settles only while the block rests on the I, so the two never meet. A frame scan at 100ms over the whole cycle at 1440x900, 1624x1205 and 390x844 finds no screen-space overlap between the block and any other part. Everything pauses when the opening leaves the screen and holds the finished pose under reduced motion. Below the opening, "What I do" opens each practice with a small scene in the same plane and materials (src/components/PillarArt): Product design, one order sent from the guest's phone, moved New, Cooking, Ready on the kitchen board and lighting the waiter's ready card (7.2s); Design systems, four token blocks in this site's own accent steps with a selector that moves along them while the button, toggle knob, card bar and tab take on the selected token (8.8s); Brand and launch, the Valora AV mark set from its own outlines standing as a sign on a plinth, with a phone publishing a post onto a feed (6s). Each fits its art box with container units, pauses off screen and rests under reduced motion; the shared art height keeps the three titles on one line. Pinned scroll scene kept: "One order, four people" inside the Plater feature. Motion elsewhere: 140ms press, 200ms hover, 320ms moves, springs only on the nav bar, Lenis smooth wheel scroll; reduced motion removes pin and smoothing and keeps every control. Order scene: one axonometric scene per station, in the pillar kit, inside a framed night panel: Table (phone menu, picked row, send bar, plates and cutlery, QR tent; 6s), Kitchen (an upright board on a counter, New/Cooking/Ready in Plater's amber/blue/green at full strength, the order's ticket crossing the columns; 7s), Waiter (the pass under a heat lamp, the plated order, the phone upright in its dock: ready, bill, paid; 6.5s), Owner (laptop with delivery zones, weekly report bars; 8s). The order is one solid lavender block with banded walls in every scene; main objects have banded solid walls. Reduced motion holds each step's moment; the scenes pause off screen.
